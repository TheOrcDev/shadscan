## The question that cannot fail out loud

shadcn's new `questionnaire` composes a multi-step form from fifteen parts.
Two of them matter more than they look.

Mark an item `required` and forget `<QuestionnaireError />`, and validation
still runs — it just has nowhere to speak. The user picks nothing, presses
Next, and the form refuses to advance without saying why. Nothing is broken
enough to notice in development, where you always fill the form in correctly.

Leave out `<QuestionnaireTitle>` and the question has no label at all: a bare
set of choices, with nothing stating what is being asked.

Neither is a matter of taste. In shadcn's own example, **5 of 5** required
items render an error and **7 of 7** items carry a title. The rule checks the
convention the component already documents by using it.

## Where the rule stops

`questionnaire` is a thin styling wrapper. The file you copy holds Tailwind
classes; the state machine, focus movement, aria wiring and error association
all live in the headless primitive underneath it.

So the rule says nothing about those. They are not yours to get wrong, and
checking them would mean auditing a package you did not write and breaking
every time it releases. It looks only at what you compose.

It is quiet in the same way elsewhere. A `required` value it cannot read
statically — `required={strict}` — is treated as unknown rather than
guessed at, because a false alarm costs more than a missed one on a rule that
is advisory by design.

## Honest about what could not be tested

The rules before this one earned their confidence on real projects. The
ButtonGroup check in 0.10.0 ran across nine of them before shipping.

This component is days old, and nobody has adopted it yet, so there was no
corpus to check against. What could be done was done: the rule was run against
shadcn's published example verbatim — where any finding at all would be a
false positive by definition — and it passed every item. Then the example was
broken on purpose, one part at a time, and it named the right item and the
right line each time.

That is a corpus of one, which is why it ships advisory and stays there until
the component sees real use.
